# ELJ Ventures · The 8-Hour Launch Master Plan

**Working reference · v2.5 · Source of truth for `index.html`**
**HTML last synced: v2.4** — *if this lags the version above, `index.html` is stale; sync at the next checkpoint. See "Pending index.html Changes" near the foot of this file.*

A working reference for launching a real business in eight hours and reaching $1M in revenue, profitably, in year one. The plan stays **model-agnostic through Hour 1** — the team picks product, service, or software during the idea-selection step, and the rest of the plan adapts. Each hour has the same shape:

- **The Issue** — what most first-time founders get wrong
- **Where to Go** — the apps, sites, and partners to use
- **Keys to Success** — the two or three things that actually matter
- **CoWork Ops Layer** — once the decisions are made and the systems are live, what Claude CoWork (or a comparable agentic tool) runs on a recurring basis, and what still requires a human

The "$10K, 4–5 people, no engineers" constraint is baked in. Everything below assumes Microsoft Office tools (Excel/Word/PowerPoint) plus a small, deliberate SaaS stack.

## Hour 1 · Idea Generation & Idea Research (and Model Pick)

### The Issue

Most first-time founders start with a demographic ("women 25–40") or a vague vibe ("eco-friendly stuff"). That's too broad to act on. Hour 1 is **three jobs**:

1. **Generate** a shortlist of 2–5 specific ideas — ideally where the team has unfair advantage from a hobby, job, or community
2. **Research** each with hard data — search volume, sales velocity, trend curves, customer complaints
3. **Pick the business model.** This is the gate to the rest of the plan. The model choice determines what Hours 2–8 actually look like.

### Idea-generation lenses (use all five)

- **Unfair advantage** — where does each of us know more than 95% of people?
- **Problem solving** — what do customers or businesses already complain about loudly?
- **Combinations** — what two existing things aren't combined and would be compelling if they were?
- **Aiding others** — what could we offer to someone already growing fast or committing serious capex?
- **Cross-geography / cross-sector** — what works elsewhere and hasn't been built here yet?

> *There are very few truly "new" ideas. The real goal is to understand the competition and find a way to differentiate, avoid, or coexist. More risk and more work create higher margins — pick the model that matches the team's risk appetite.*

### The model gate (decide before Hour 2)

| If you pick → | Hours 2–8 look like |
|---|---|
| **Physical product, DTC ecom** | Shopify-centric: hero SKU, paid social, 3PL or self-fulfill |
| **Physical product, retail/wholesale** | Shopify + Faire/Abound; less ad spend, more buyer outreach |
| **Service (managed)** | Booking + Stripe Billing + a clear scope-of-work template |
| **Marketplace** | Sharetribe or Webflow + two-sided supply/demand seeding |
| **Software / info app** | Bubble, Lovable, or no-code; subscription billing via Paddle/Lemon Squeezy |
| **Community / content** | Circle, Mighty, or Substack; revenue via paid tier + sponsorships |

### Where to Go

**Idea generation sources**
- [IdeaBrowser](https://www.ideabrowser.com/) — AI-curated business ideas with market data & opportunity scoring (best single site in this category)
- [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/) — long-form trend reports
- [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/ideas) — case studies of founders hitting revenue milestones
- [Failory](https://www.failory.com/) — failed and successful startup teardowns
- [Validator AI](https://www.validatorai.com/) — give it a one-line idea, get a critique
- Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini — *"20 niche [model] ideas where buyer is [persona], market $50–500M, [model-fit constraints]"*

**Entrepreneurial media** — Indie Hackers, r/Entrepreneur, Show HN, Product Hunt, My First Million pod, The Hustle, Startup Ideas Podcast, Not Boring

**Niche & category research** — Jungle Scout, Helium 10, Google Trends, Exploding Topics, Glimpse, Trend Hunter, CB Insights

**Social listening & pain mining** — GummySearch, F5Bot, Syften, Devi AI, PainOnSocial, SubReddit Stats, X Pro

### Keys to success

1. End Hour 1 with **2–5 named ideas with one-line problem statements** — not 20 vague directions
2. **Pick the model before Hour 2.** Hours 2–8 branch differently for each. Hour 1 is the only place to defer this.
3. The unfair-advantage prompt beats any database. Sit the four founders down and ask: *what do each of us pay to solve right now?*

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 1

**Cadence:** Daily (or weekly) · **Trigger:** Recurring schedule once a niche is picked

**CoWork can do:**
- Pull top posts from 5–10 target subreddits matching pain-point keywords
- Pull Reddit and X mentions of competitor brands or category keywords
- Summarize into a digest (Word doc, Notion page, or email) with direct quotes for ad copy
- Flag new posts containing "I wish there was," "is there a tool that," "recommend"
- Maintain a running spreadsheet of customer language tagged by pain category

**CoWork can't:**
- Make the strategic call about which idea to pursue
- Pick the business model
- Interview customers or do live conversations

**Setup cost:** ~45 minutes once. **Daily human time after that:** ~5 minutes to skim the digest.

## Hour 2 · Validate Demand & Pick Hero Offering

### The Issue

"Blue ocean" niches usually mean no customers, not untapped gold. Confirm money is already moving in the category. Pick **one hero offering** — the thing you're known for — plus 1–2 complementary items. Launching with 50 SKUs (or 50 service tiers, or 50 features) splits attention and budget; launching with one lets you test, learn, and double down.

The validation question is the same regardless of model: **are people already spending money here, and can we capture some of it differently?**

### Where to Go (by model)

**Physical product** — Amazon Best Sellers (free), Etsy Search (handmade), Keepa (Amazon price/rank history), TikTok Creative Center, Google Trends

**Service** — Upwork/Fiverr category search (are people already paying for this?), Reddit "hiring a [provider]" search, LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters

**Software / info app** — competitor reviews on G2/Capterra/Product Hunt (what do users complain about?), search volume on the category keyword in Google Keyword Planner

**Marketplace** — Are there active sub-communities on Reddit/Discord/FB Groups where supply and demand already trade informally? That informal trade is your validation.

### Keys to success

1. **One hero offering** — name it, price it, write the one-line value prop today
2. Demand signal must be *current* — last 90 days, not "this was hot in 2022"
3. Pair validation with Hour 1's social listening — 200 people asking for something adjacent with no clean answer is your green light

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 2

**Cadence:** Weekly · **Trigger:** Recurring schedule + manual ad-hoc for new hero candidates

**CoWork can do:**
- Track competitor pricing on the hero product/offering weekly (scrape competitor pages, log in spreadsheet)
- Track competitor review velocity (count of new Amazon/G2/Trustpilot reviews per week) as a sales proxy
- Pull Google Trends snapshot for the category each Friday
- Compile a "validation dashboard" Word/Excel digest the team reviews Monday
- Flag when a competitor changes price, runs a promo, or releases a new variant

**CoWork can't:**
- Place test buys on competitor products (judgment call + budget)
- Talk to customers in person or on video
- Decide that validation has "passed"

**Setup cost:** ~30 minutes once. **Weekly human time:** ~15 minutes to review.

## Hour 3 · Sourcing / Building the Offering

### The Issue

Where the offering comes from determines margin, speed-to-customer, quality control, and how much capital is needed upfront. The shape of "sourcing" depends entirely on the model picked in Hour 1:

- **Physical product** — print-on-demand, dropshipping, wholesale, or custom manufacturing (covered below)
- **Service business** — booking front-end, productizing the offer, provider sourcing (see `service_app_sourcing.html`)
- **App / software** — visual no-code builder (Bubble, Glide, Softr, Adalo), AI prompt-to-app (Lovable, Bolt, v0), or — eventually — engineering (see `service_app_sourcing.html` for week-one paths, `app_build_paths.html` for the longer-horizon route)

### Where to Go — Physical Product

**Print-on-demand** — [Printify](https://printify.com/) (cheapest, widest), [Printful](https://www.printful.com/) (better QC), [Gelato](https://www.gelato.com/) (best international)

**Dropshipping** — [Spocket](https://www.spocket.co/) (US/EU suppliers), [AutoDS](https://www.autods.com/), [Zendrop](https://www.zendrop.com/), [DSers](https://www.dsers.com/) (AliExpress)

**Wholesale** — [Faire](https://www.faire.com/) (net-60 for new retailers), [Abound](https://helloabound.com/), [Tundra](https://www.tundra.com/) (no fees), [Wonnda](https://wonnda.com/) (EU + private label)

**Custom manufacturing** — [Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/), [Pietra](https://pietrastudio.com/) (curated beauty/jewelry/apparel), [Maker's Row](https://makersrow.com/) (US)

### Where to Go — Service Business

See `service_app_sourcing.html` § Service business for the full breakdown. Quick summary:

- **Booking + payments + intake** — [Cal.com](https://cal.com/) (open-source default, free tier generous), [Acuity Scheduling](https://acuityscheduling.com/) (built for client-facing service businesses), [SavvyCal](https://savvycal.com/), [TidyCal](https://tidycal.com/) (lifetime deal)
- **Productizing the offer** — [Notion](https://www.notion.so/) / Google Docs / Loom for SOPs, [Dubsado](https://www.dubsado.com/) or [HoneyBook](https://www.honeybook.com/) for client portal/contracts/invoicing
- **Provider sourcing** (if not delivering yourself) — Upwork, [Contra](https://contra.com/), [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/), Fiverr Pro, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, niche Slack/Discord communities

> **The leverage move for services:** productize the offer. Bespoke services cap at hours × rate × team size — mathematically can't reach $1M with 4 people on hourly work. Fixed-scope, fixed-price packages are how you escape that ceiling.

### Where to Go — App / Software (week-one paths)

See `service_app_sourcing.html` § No-code builders and § AI prompt-to-app for the full breakdown. Quick summary:

**Tier 1 — Visual no-code builders** (mature, predictable, slower to ship, easier to maintain)
- [Bubble](https://bubble.io/) — most capable for complex web apps; SaaS, marketplaces, internal tools with real logic
- [Glide](https://www.glideapps.com/), [Softr](https://www.softr.io/) — spreadsheet-driven; fastest if data is structured; great for portals and internal tools
- [Adalo](https://www.adalo.com/), [FlutterFlow](https://flutterflow.io/) — native mobile apps published to App Store / Play Store
- [Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/) — purpose-built marketplace engine
- [Circle](https://circle.so/), [Mighty Networks](https://www.mightynetworks.com/) — paid communities

**Tier 2 — AI prompt-to-app** (fast prototype-to-real-code; the "80% problem" applies — last 20% is hard)
- [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/) — polished UI output, Supabase backend, Stripe built-in, GitHub export with two-way sync. Best balance.
- [Bolt.new](https://bolt.new/) — browser-based, instant preview, strong frontend output
- [v0](https://v0.dev/) — Vercel's design-system-aware builder; best for design-forward UIs
- [Replit Agent](https://replit.com/) — full-stack with AI pair programmer; needs more technical comfort

**Tier 3 — AI-assisted real code** (power option for teams with one technically-comfortable founder)
- [Claude Code](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-code), [Cursor](https://www.cursor.com/) — produce dramatically better code than prompt-to-app tools, no platform lock-in

### Keys to success

1. **For products: order samples.** Never list a product you haven't held. Allocate $100–500 from working capital.
2. **For services: productize from day one.** Write the pricing page like you're selling a product, not your time.
3. **For apps: pick your tier consciously.** Tier 1 (no-code) trades speed for durability. Tier 2 (AI prompt-to-app) trades durability for speed. Pick based on whether month four matters more than week one.
4. Lock in **unit economics** before Hour 4 — landed cost, fees, processing — so the price in Hour 5 actually clears margin.

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 3

**Cadence:** Weekly + event-driven · **Trigger:** Inventory thresholds, supplier emails, plan-limit alerts, error logs

The exact shape of the CoWork loop depends on the path chosen — see the relevant supplement for the model-specific version:

- **Physical product** (default loop, shown below): supplier inbox monitoring, vendor reliability scorecard, landed-cost comparison, reorder PO drafting
- **Service business** (see `service_app_sourcing.html` § Service): intake triage, SOP-template matching, weekly engagement-hours-vs-scope review, contractor reliability scoring
- **No-code app** (see `service_app_sourcing.html` § No-code): plan-cap usage tracking, integration health monitoring, data-table hygiene audits
- **AI prompt-to-app** (see `service_app_sourcing.html` § AI prompt-to-app): credit burn tracking, auth/security regression monitoring, error log summarization

**Default loop (physical product) — CoWork can do:**
- Monitor supplier inboxes for price changes, MOQ updates, lead-time changes
- Track sample arrival vs. promised dates; build a vendor reliability scorecard
- Compare landed cost across multiple suppliers in a running Excel sheet
- Draft re-order POs from a template when inventory drops below threshold
- Pull Alibaba/Faire/Spocket new-listing emails into a "potential sources" doc

**CoWork can't:**
- Negotiate price with a supplier
- Approve a wire transfer or PO
- Inspect a physical sample
- Make a judgment call on quality

**Setup cost:** ~1 hour once. **Weekly human time:** ~20 minutes.

## Hour 4 · Brand, Messaging & Store/Front-End Build

### The Issue

Founders waste hours on logos and color palettes. The brand at launch needs four things: a **name people can spell**, a **domain you own**, a **one-line value prop**, and **visuals that look intentional**. You will redesign everything within six months once you know who actually buys. Don't optimize before there's data.

Service businesses don't need Shopify — info apps use creator platforms, marketplaces use Webflow + Sharetribe, communities use Circle/Mighty, managed services use a simple Cal.com booking page + a Notion site or Squarespace one-pager.

### Where to Go

**Domain registration** — [Cloudflare Registrar](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/) (at-cost), [Namecheap](https://www.namecheap.com/), [Porkbun](https://porkbun.com/)

**Logo & brand identity** — [Looka](https://looka.com/), [Canva](https://www.canva.com/), [Brandmark](https://brandmark.io/)

**Store / front-end platform (by model)**
- Physical product → [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/) (default), [Squarespace Commerce](https://www.squarespace.com/ecommerce-website), [BigCommerce](https://www.bigcommerce.com/) (B2B/high SKU)
- Managed service → Cal.com + Squarespace/Webflow one-pager + Stripe
- Marketplace → Sharetribe, or Webflow + Memberstack
- Community → [Circle](https://circle.so/), [Mighty Networks](https://www.mightynetworks.com/)
- Info app / SaaS → Bubble, Lovable, or Webflow + Stripe Billing

See `build_paths.html` for the Shopify build comments. See `app_build_paths.html` for software.

### Keys to success

1. **Buy the domain at Cloudflare or Porkbun** — not the platform's upsell. Saves $40–80/year and keeps DNS portable.
2. **Spend < 90 minutes on the logo.** Looka or Canva, move on.
3. **One-line value prop, written down.** Every page, ad, and email pulls from this line.

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 4

**Cadence:** Weekly + event-driven · **Trigger:** Recurring + domain/DNS/SSL alerts

**CoWork can do:**
- Monitor site uptime (UptimeRobot-style) and email the team on outages
- Track domain renewal dates and DNS/SSL expiry — flag 30 days out
- Run a weekly Lighthouse/PageSpeed check on the homepage and product pages
- Diff the homepage against last week's version and flag unintended changes
- Compile competitor homepage screenshots monthly for design reference

**CoWork can't:**
- Edit the live site without explicit per-change approval
- Change DNS records or domain settings
- Choose a logo or write the value prop

**Setup cost:** ~30 minutes once. **Weekly human time:** ~10 minutes.

## Hour 5 · Product Pages & Payments

### The Issue

This is where stores leak money. Bad photos, feature-led copy ("100% cotton, 200gsm") instead of benefit-led ("the t-shirt that doesn't pill after three washes"), broken checkout, no clear shipping or returns info. Customers bounce in seconds.

For services, "product page" is the **offer page** — what you do, who it's for, what it costs, how to start. For software, "checkout" is recurring billing — Stripe Billing, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, or Memberstack.

### Where to Go

**Payment processing** — [Shopify Payments](https://www.shopify.com/payments), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/), [PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/us/business), [Shop Pay](https://shop.app/shop-pay)

**Subscription/recurring** — Stripe Billing, [Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/), [Lemon Squeezy](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/), [Recharge](https://rechargepayments.com/) (Shopify subs)

**Product photography (AI)** — [Pebblely](https://pebblely.com/), [Claid.ai](https://claid.ai/), [Canva](https://www.canva.com/)

**Copywriting** — Claude or ChatGPT for first drafts (always with the human voice on top), [Copy.ai](https://www.copy.ai/), [Anyword](https://anyword.com/)

### Keys to success

1. **Benefit-led headline, feature-led body.** Lead with what changes for the customer, support with specs underneath.
2. **Photo standard: 6+ images per SKU** — hero, scale, in-use, detail, packaging, comparison. AI-generated is fine for launch; replace with real shots once selling.
3. **Test checkout on mobile, on a real phone, with a real card.** Do this before driving any traffic.

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 5

**Cadence:** Daily + event-driven · **Trigger:** Sales spikes, failed checkouts, new product launch

**CoWork can do:**
- Monitor Stripe/Shopify for failed checkouts and surface a daily count + top failure reasons
- Pull abandoned cart counts and email the team a daily "leak report"
- Generate first-draft product copy and meta descriptions from a spec sheet
- A/B test result tracking — pull conversion-rate data by variant into a weekly digest
- Flag products with declining conversion week-over-week

**CoWork can't:**
- Approve a price change
- Edit the live product page without per-change approval
- Make the call on which variant wins an A/B test (still a human judgment, especially at low volume)

**Setup cost:** ~45 minutes once. **Daily human time:** ~5 minutes.

## Hour 6 · Operations, Inventory & Fulfillment

### The Issue

Most first-timers underestimate this. "Operations" means everything between a customer clicking "buy" and them opening the box. **Pack orders yourself** (cheap, slow, doesn't scale) or **outsource to a 3PL** (they hold inventory, pick, pack, ship). Most 3PLs have minimums that don't make sense until 50–100+ orders/month — plan to self-fulfill at first and migrate when packing eats more than 5 hrs/week.

For services, "operations" is **provider onboarding, quality control, dispute handling, payouts** — the four of us coordinate, never deliver. For software, "operations" is uptime, support response time, billing dispute handling.

### Where to Go

**Full-service 3PLs** — [ShipBob](https://www.shipbob.com/), [ShipMonk](https://www.shipmonk.com/), [Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/), [Saltbox](https://saltbox.com/). See `3pl_costs.html` for the cost breakdown.

**Amazon-integrated** — [Amazon FBA](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon), [Amazon MCF](https://sell.amazon.com/grow/multi-channel-fulfillment), [Shopify Fulfillment Network](https://www.shopify.com/fulfillment)

**Inventory management** — [Cin7](https://www.cin7.com/), [Katana](https://katanamrp.com/) (makers), [Zoho Inventory](https://www.zoho.com/inventory/), [Inventory Planner](https://www.inventory-planner.com/), [Stocky](https://apps.shopify.com/stocky)

**Shipping & returns** — [Pirate Ship](https://www.pirateship.com/) (cheapest USPS, free), [ShipStation](https://www.shipstation.com/), [Shippo](https://goshippo.com/), [Loop Returns](https://www.loopreturns.com/), [AfterShip Returns](https://www.aftership.com/returns)

### Keys to success

1. **Self-fulfill until packing > 5 hrs/week** across the team. Then migrate to a 3PL.
2. **Returns policy on the site from day one.** Friction here kills repeat purchase.
3. **Re-order threshold = lead time × weekly velocity × 1.5.** Set this in Excel, monitor weekly.

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 6

**Cadence:** Daily · **Trigger:** Recurring + inventory thresholds + delivery exceptions

**CoWork can do:**
- Daily inventory snapshot — current stock vs. reorder threshold per SKU
- Pull tracking from ShipStation/Pirate Ship and flag any orders stalled > 48 hours
- Customer-facing shipping delay drafts (held for human approval before send)
- Returns reason summary (weekly) — feeds back into product or page improvements
- 3PL invoice reconciliation — does the bill match the orders shipped?
- Generate POs to suppliers when threshold breached (human approves and sends)

**CoWork can't:**
- Approve a 3PL invoice
- Place an order with a supplier
- Physically inspect a shipment
- Authorize a refund or exchange outside policy

**Setup cost:** ~1.5 hours once. **Daily human time:** ~15 minutes.

## Hour 7 · Legal, Finance & Customer Operations

### The Issue

Running revenue through personal accounts is a tax nightmare and creates personal liability. You need an **LLC, EIN, business bank account, basic terms and privacy pages, sales-tax handling** (vastly more complex after the 2018 Wayfair ruling), and a **customer support system** before the first complaint hits. **Most of this is already done for ELJ Ventures** — Hour 0 covered the formation work. Hour 7 is the operating layer on top.

### Where to Go

**Bookkeeping** — [Xero](https://www.xero.com/) (clean, ecom-friendly), [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/) (most CPAs know it), [Bench](https://www.bench.co/) (done-for-you, ~$300/mo)

**Customer support** — [Gorgias](https://www.gorgias.com/) (Shopify-native), [Help Scout](https://www.helpscout.com/) (generalist), [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/) (AI-first), [Tidio](https://www.tidio.com/) (free tier)

**Sales tax** — [TaxJar](https://www.taxjar.com/), [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/), [Numeral](https://www.numeralhq.com/)

**Terms / Privacy** — [Termly](https://termly.io/), [iubenda](https://www.iubenda.com/), or a $200 lawyer pass once revenue is moving

**Already-decided alternatives** (kept for reference) — Northwest Registered Agent, Mercury, Relay

### Keys to success

1. **Terms of Service + Privacy Policy + Refund Policy live before paid traffic.** Templates are fine for launch.
2. **Sales-tax nexus tracking starts at $0 in revenue.** Set TaxJar or Numeral on a free tier from day one; activate auto-file when a state's threshold is crossed.
3. **Support inbox response < 24 hours.** Even a "we got it, we're on it" cuts negative review risk by half.

### CoWork Ops Layer — Hour 7

**Cadence:** Daily (support) + Weekly (books) + Quarterly (legal/tax) · **Trigger:** Inbound tickets, transaction count, threshold breaches

**CoWork can do:**
- Triage support inbox — categorize by topic, draft replies for human approval, escalate the rest
- Weekly P&L pull from QuickBooks/Xero with variance vs. last week and vs. plan
- Monthly sales-tax-nexus check — total revenue and orders per state vs. each state's threshold
- Quarterly reminder + checklist for state filings (registered agent reports, franchise tax)
- Draft refund/return responses based on policy (human approves before send)
- Weekly review summary — Trustpilot/Google/Yotpo ratings and flagged negative reviews

**CoWork can't:**
- Send a support reply or refund without human approval
- File a tax return or state report
- Modify the Terms of Service / Privacy Policy
- Sign a contract or NDA

**Setup cost:** ~2 hours once. **Daily human time on support:** ~20 minutes. **Weekly on books:** ~15 minutes.

## Hour 8 · Launch, Marketing & Content Engine

### The Issue

Most work happens *after* launch, but the infrastructure has to be live before the first ad dollar. Two distinct channels:

- **Organic content** — free posts on your social, algorithm-distributed
- **Paid ads** — you pay Meta, TikTok, Google, Pinterest to put creative in front of specific people

Different goals, different creative. Most brands run both: paid drives sales now, organic builds an audience for retargeting. Service marketing leans on Google Search, SEO, LinkedIn, and case studies over scroll-stopping creative.

> ⚠️ **Klaviyo and Google Analytics 4 must be live before any traffic.** You cannot recover events that weren't tracked.

> ⚠️ **The AI content saturation trap.** Brands winning aren't posting the most AI content — they're posting AI content with a real human point of view layered on top. Use these tools to remove busywork, not to replace having something to say.

### Where to Go
